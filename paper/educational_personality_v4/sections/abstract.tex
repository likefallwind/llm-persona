\begin{abstract}
Do language models exhibit recurring teaching tendencies, and what transfers
when students express themselves differently? We study educational personality
as observable action profiles, separating defaults from student-state responses.
Following a million-record archive audit, two prospective stages generate
10,440 replies from five deployments, using automated coding without human
annotation. On 32 new problems, locked forecasts reduce Brier loss by 0.0913
for answer revelation and 0.1297 for reasoning elicitation; student-state
interactions add 0.0097 for affect acknowledgement. An expression extension
reuses those problems and forecasts. Default gains persist, but conditional
acknowledgement gain changes from +0.0125 under contemporary original wording
to -0.0037 under explicit synonyms and -0.0063 under implicit cues. Explicit
instructions make demanded actions converge while permissible accompanying
actions remain different. In 3,584 archived benchmark replies from seven
historical deployments, revelation and elicitation model gains are 0.0468 and
0.0419 under general tutoring, but 0.0008 and 0.0004 under explicit probing.
Thus, cross-problem success does not establish cross-expression transfer, and
predictive differences depend on instructional constraints. The evidence
supports recurring action profiles with specified transfer boundaries, not
psychological traits or learning benefits.
\end{abstract}
