\begin{abstract}
Artificial intelligence (AI) tutors can answer the same question correctly yet differ in how they help
students. We investigate whether language models have recurring teaching
tendencies, which we call \emph{educational personality}, and when those
tendencies remain predictable or change with instructions. Drawing on selected
subsets of an archive of over one million educational evaluation records,
we combine exploratory analyses with targeted experiments and predictions
fixed before collecting new responses. We find recurring model differences
in some teaching behaviors, such as giving answers and inviting reasoning,
while other measures show shared responses or less consistent differences.
Some differences help predict responses to new problems even under the tested
changes in student wording, but this predictive value depends on the behavior
and input conditions. Explicit instructions can align the requested action
without making models behave alike throughout the response. These findings
give educators a basis for comparing AI tutors through their teaching habits,
when those habits can be anticipated, and how they respond to instructions,
alongside task performance. For developers, the results motivate evaluating each
model's behavior to identify where prompts suffice and where additional
system support needs to be tested.
\end{abstract}
