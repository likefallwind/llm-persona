\section{Conclusion}

These deployments exhibit recurring teaching tendencies, but their transfer
boundaries differ. Model defaults predict actions on new problems, and the
answer-revelation and reasoning-elicitation defaults retain their gains under
tested changes in student expression. The acknowledgement state profile,
although predictive on new problems with the original wording, does not retain
its incremental gain under new expressions. Explicit teaching instructions
further make demanded actions converge while allowing different accompanying
behavior. A defensible educational-personality claim must therefore specify
which actions recur, under which input changes, and beyond which contextual
baseline. These results support prompt-dependent action profiles; they do not
establish immutable human-like types, emotional understanding, or learning
benefits. Transfer to archived benchmark dialogue remains untested in this draft.
