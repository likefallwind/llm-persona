\title{Steering Is Not Personalization:\\
Stable Behavioral Differences and the Adaptation Gap in AI Tutors}
\author{Anonymous ACL submission}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Prompting can make an AI tutor ask more questions, reveal fewer answers, or sound
more supportive. It is tempting to call this personalization. We show that it is
usually something narrower: a reliable shift in behavior that need not fit the
learner. Using same-item responses from a large educational benchmark archive,
we first ask whether models differ at all, which differences recur across tasks,
and how a shared pedagogy prompt changes them. We then test whether the induced
behavior agrees with human teacher actions, reflects learner-state diagnosis, or
supports context-conditioned action selection. Models do exhibit recurring
defaults. Help directness transports across teaching tasks and converges with
independently inferred teacher actions; expressed confidence retains a stable
ordering across four sources. Yet the same prompt moves every model sharply
toward elicitation and away from direct help, with model-specific movement and
residual model identity. This steering is not personalization. The prompt raises
aggregate teacher-action agreement but suppresses direct instruction when human
teachers chose telling. Models realize an assigned ASK or EXPLAIN action almost
perfectly, while tested selectors remain near chance. Several attractive trait
interpretations also fail: steerable actionability does not recur, relational
communion largely restates warmth, and questionnaire agreeableness does not
predict open behavior. AI-tutor evaluation should therefore treat behavior as a
model--instruction--context interaction and separate default style, steering,
action selection, and learning outcomes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

People interacting with AI tutors quickly notice that systems teach differently.
Given the same learner and problem, one model explains, another asks a question,
and a third answers with unusual certainty. Before asking whether such
differences are stable, a more basic question is necessary: after the item and
generation setup are controlled, do models exhibit systematic differences in
educational behavior at all? If they do, a second question follows: are these
differences properties that recur across teaching tasks, or artifacts of one
benchmark, one prompt, or a generic authorship signature?

Most language-model personality research begins with human inventories or an
assigned persona \citep{jiang-etal-2024-personallm,lee-etal-2025-trait}. Those
methods show that a model can produce trait-consistent answers, but they do not
show that the same ordering appears when the model actually teaches. Inventory
scores also change with wording and option order \citep{shu-etal-2024-dont}, and
recent work questions whether human psychometric constructs retain their meaning
for models \citep{han-etal-2025-personality-illusion,zierahn-etal-2026-arent-human,contreras-2026-native-psychometric}.

Education makes the distinction consequential and testable. A tutor must decide
whether to tell or elicit, whether to reveal an answer or preserve learner work,
how much information to introduce, and how firmly to state a judgment. These
choices can be compared across repeated teaching situations and against human
teacher actions or learner-state labels. They also expose a distinction that is
easy to miss in prompt engineering: reliably inducing a teaching move is not the
same as selecting the right move for this learner now. A question can be useful
after a productive error and obstructive when direct instruction is needed.

We therefore ask six questions. Do models differ in educational behavior under
matched conditions? Which differences recur across teaching tasks, and do any
extend beyond education? Are they distinct from length, formatting, and generic
authorship cues? How much does a shared pedagogy prompt move them? Do models
respond to that prompt in the same way? Finally, does a recurring or induced
style predict context-appropriate teaching? We use ``educational personality''
only as shorthand for recurring observable tendencies in these settings, not
for an inner state or a human-equivalent trait.

The study was made possible by an unusual data opportunity. Our EduBenchmark
archive contains same-item responses from multiple models across teaching and
generic tasks, generated under shared harnesses. We first use that archive to
screen candidate differences while controlling for item content and generic
authorship cues. We then apply paired pedagogy prompts, blinded semantic ratings,
human teacher actions, human-gold learner diagnoses, and one small cross-domain
affiliation pilot. This archive-first design lets new model calls test specific
claims rather than manufacture a personality result from a newly chosen scale.

The findings yield one central distinction. Models have recurring defaults, and
a concise prompt can strongly and reliably bias their behavior toward a common
teaching style. But a fixed behavioral shift is not personalized education. The
question-first prompt improves aggregate action agreement while harming the
subset where human teachers chose telling; adaptive-looking prose does not add
learner-state information; and models that execute prescribed actions nearly
perfectly still struggle to select them from context. The appropriate object of
evaluation is therefore neither the model nor the prompt in isolation, but the
model--instruction system acting in a learner context.

\section{Related Work}

Behavior-first psychometrics includes projective stimuli and construct-matched
contexts \citep{wang-etal-2026-genpt,kocielnik-etal-2026-rethinking}. Cross-task
work studies assigned-persona consistency across elicitation formats
\citep{reusens-etal-2025-persona-consistency}. We audit unassigned shared-harness
tutor defaults under task, fingerprint, intervention, and external-criterion
controls. Our targeted bridge uses public-domain IPIP items
\citep{goldberg-etal-2006-ipip} only as one measurement route, not as ground
truth for model behavior.

MathDial defines teacher moves and premature solution reveal
\citep{macina-etal-2023-mathdial}; MRBench and MathTutorBench organize open-ended
tutoring capabilities
\citep{maurya-etal-2025-mrbench,macina-etal-2025-mathtutorbench}. LongTutor
separates historical evidence, knowledge-state diagnosis, and adaptive teaching
\citep{li-etal-2026-longtutor}. Pedagogical Alignment and StratL train or optimize
a predefined scaffolded policy \citep{sonkar-etal-2024-pedagogical,puech-etal-2025-steering};
prompt moderation can also harm relative to an unmoderated tutor
\citep{steindl-etal-2025-prompt}. Closest to our context-sensitivity question,
\citet{borchers-shou-2025-adaptivity} find limited adaptivity under learner-context
ablations. Tutor-persona steering \citep{lee-etal-2026-tutor-personas} and
theory-grounded runtime control \citep{nkambou-etal-2026-pedrag} ask how a desired
policy can be induced, while PATS adapts strategies to student personality
\citep{rooein-etal-2026-pats}. We instead measure recurring deployed-system
defaults, audit their construct validity, and then apply the same intervention.
Work on tutor-strategy prediction \citep{ikram-etal-2025-strategy} and feedback
failure localization \citep{yasir-etal-2026-feedback} further motivates reporting
action-specific errors instead of one aggregate tutor score.
Strategy-before-generation is itself established: prior work jointly predicts
tutor strategies and responses \citep{wang-etal-2023-strategize}, while Tutor
CoPilot deliberately retains human strategy selection in a live system
\citep{wang-etal-2025-tutor-copilot}. SLOW explicitly separates learner-state
reasoning from action selection \citep{wei-etal-2026-slow}. Our routing test is
therefore a controlled falsification of prompt-only modularization, not a claim
to invent a two-stage tutor.

Answer withholding is already studied under pedagogical jailbreaks and
multi-turn adversarial student pressure
\citep{zhao-etal-2026-shape,zhao-etal-2026-answer-leakage}. Real deployment data
also show that students commonly seek answers and that context can outweigh
intended system design \citep{kobler-etal-2026-students}. Our learner-action
analysis is therefore not a first leakage or steering claim, and policy alignment
is not treated as learning benefit.

More generally, IHEval finds sharp degradation when instruction priorities
conflict \citep{zhang-etal-2025-iheval}. Whether an explicit learner request
survives system-level policy clauses is therefore an instruction-hierarchy test,
not evidence of empathy, learner understanding, or pedagogical appropriateness.

Attribution and one aggregate score are insufficient: lexical features identify
generators across domains, while general helpfulness can reverse between judges
\citep{mcgovern-etal-2025-fingerprints,fan-etal-2026-helpfulness}. Prompted students are
also not assumed to measure learning: recent evaluations find weak linguistic,
behavioral, and cognitive fidelity \citep{scarlatos-etal-2026-simulated}. We
therefore treat objective diagnosis and evidence retrieval as prerequisite
competence and reserve learning gain for prospective work.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Transparent features and transport}

We deterministically count length, structure, formatting, questions, address
terms, praise, encouragement, hedges, imperatives, history references, answer
reveal, explanation, and diagnosis markers. Features are centered across models
within exact item and standardized within benchmark. Multinomial logistic
regression predicts model identity while holding out an entire task family. The
same protocol on generic tasks is a negative control: strong generic attribution
prevents relabeling an authorship fingerprint as pedagogy.

For paired prompts we subtract the generic-arm feature from the pedagogy-arm
feature for the same context and model. Bootstrap resampling preserves the six
correlated responses within each context. We measure both directional
model-by-model effects and cross-model profile dispersion.

\subsection{Quality, actions, and learner state}

Quality prediction holds out one candidate model at a time. Exact-item effects
form the baseline; length and policy features are nested additions. Byte-identical
responses scored by two judges provide a judge swap. A separate set of 482 expert
positive/negative pairs, each shown in both orders, audits three judges.

Three TF--IDF linear-SVM variants are trained on 18,541 human teacher turns, with
five-fold splits grouped by math problem. Generated responses are then compared
with the human next move. LongTutor analyses join teaching, diagnosis, and
evidence tasks by exact history and model. Held-out-model prediction includes
exact-history effects before teaching scores are added.

\subsection{Candidate educational tendencies}

We chose dimensions from decisions that a tutor must make, not from human trait
labels. Four requirements constrained the set: a dimension had to be observable
in an open response, educationally interpretable, comparable across heterogeneous
tasks, and falsifiable through recurrence or an external criterion. This yields
four families.

\emph{Assistance allocation} separates help directness (revealing an answer,
step, or explanation), elicitation (advancing through questions or prompts), and
autonomy support (leaving consequential reasoning or choice to the learner).
These are related but not opposites: a turn may explain and then elicit, and a
question need not preserve learner control. \emph{Learner orientation} separates
affective warmth, diagnostic specificity (naming evidence or a misconception),
and personalization (adapting content or strategy to this learner); warm language
alone need not diagnose or adapt. \emph{Instructional management} covers the
information burden imposed in one turn, directive agency, and whether a concrete
next action is supplied. \emph{Epistemic expression} separates cautious wording,
expressed confidence, revision, and abstention; none is treated as correctness or
calibration. Together these families cover how much help is given, how the learner
is engaged, how the interaction is advanced, and how knowledge status is
communicated.

A candidate counts as recurring only when its model ordering transports across
task sources. Reliable annotation, cross-task recurrence, and independent
educational validation are separate evidence levels. Correlations among six
model configurations are descriptive and are not interpreted as population-level
latent factors. Four frozen sources provide confidence and revision measures and
five provide unanswerable-abstention rates.

\subsection{Blinded semantic panel and frozen hierarchy}

The deterministic sample initially contained 360 context--arm batches and 2,160
responses: 40 LongTutor, 80 standard MathDial pairs, 40 hard pairs, and 80
Socratic contexts. Three judges coded eight bipolar 1--5 dimensions: help
directness, elicitation, autonomy support, affective warmth, diagnostic
specificity, personalization, cognitive load, and epistemic caution. Model
identity was hidden and response order independently randomized by judge and
batch. Confirmatory scores are judge medians.

One DeepSeek annotation remained unavailable after three rounds of three
attempts. Before formal analysis and without inspecting the affected scores, we
excluded that generic batch and its prespecified pedagogy counterpart for all
judges. The complete-case panel therefore contains 358 batches, 2,148 responses,
and 1,074 annotations (0.56\% technical attrition).

All eight dimensions remain reported. A frozen evidence hierarchy requires
(i) ICC(3,k)$\geq .60$, adequate range, leave-one-judge profile
$\rho\geq .70$, and no prompt sign reversal; (ii) nonzero exact-context model
variance plus cross-task ICC or median task correlation $\geq .50$; and (iii) an
independent quality/action, prompt/action, or BH-corrected human-gold diagnosis
criterion. These gates keep reliable measurement, cross-task recurrence, and
external behavioral validation as separate claims.

\subsection{Prospective component intervention}

Before inspecting any generated response or effect, we froze 32 synthetic
elementary-math problems and a complete $2\times2\times2$ intervention over
question-first, answer-reveal, and warm-tone system clauses. Each cell is crossed
with learner requests for direct help or exploration and five reachable model
routes (2,560 calls). Deterministic detectors score whether the first
sentence-like segment is a question, whether the correct answer is revealed, and
whether a frozen encouragement lexicon appears. Every cell, model, family,
secondary surface outcome, and
two- and three-way interaction remains reported.

A component must exceed its registered target-effect threshold (.60 for question
and answer; .30 for tone), be positive in every model, and have a target effect at
least twice its largest primary cross-effect. Learner-request adaptation has
separate .10 thresholds. The parent queue was lexicographic, so, still blind to
outcomes, we froze an eight-problem replay with byte-identical prompts,
model-specific SHA-256 order, and exact balance of all 16 request--policy cells in
every consecutive block. A claim is order-robust only if both panels pass and
every model is positive in the matching parent subset and replication; the replay
can only downgrade a claim.

After seeing aggregate effects, we froze a downgrade-only detector audit. An
outcome-independent hash sample contains 480 responses (320 parent, 160 replay).
Three blinded judges label all 48 ten-response batches without model, factor,
detector, or effect information. Validation requires majority coverage $\geq
.95$, balanced accuracy $\geq .90$ with bootstrap lower bound $\geq .80$, and
Cohen's $\kappa\geq .70$; failure restricts reporting to the literal detector.

\subsection{Inference, multiplicity, and freeze timing}

The inferential unit follows the claim (Table~\ref{tab:data}). Cross-task
attribution reports an unweighted macro-average over held-out families. Prompt
intervals resample underlying contexts while retaining all model-linked rows;
judge calls are first reduced to response medians and never counted as
independent educational cases. Quality models report every held-out-model fold,
and exact sign tests treat folds, not responses, as replicates. Prompt tests use
BH correction over the 48 model $\times$ dimension contrasts within each
difficulty; semantic diagnosis uses within-outcome BH correction.

The multi-judge plan was frozen at 311/1,080 annotations, all from the first
judge and before any second- or third-judge result. Single-judge directions had
been inspected, so this is a prospective multi-judge freeze, not a full
preregistration. Manuscript decision thresholds were fixed at 637/1,080, before
the third judge and all LongTutor annotations; a later multiplicity amendment
also preceded all semantic LongTutor results. Analyzer hashes and the technical-
attrition amendment are retained in the audit trail.

\subsection{General-personality bridge}

Before new generation, a theory-first archive audit mapped Big Five, HEXACO
honesty--humility, Dark Triad, Schwartz values, adjacent validity constructs,
measurement routes, and stability perturbations to existing tasks. Candidate
language profiles had to recur outside tutoring, transport to default tutoring,
and survive verbosity and organizational-style controls. The audit could select,
but not confirm, one prospective follow-up.

It selected affiliation. We froze five model configurations, twelve matched
educational/non-educational conflicts, four conditions (default, irrelevant
interface context, high affiliation, and low affiliation), ten IPIP
agreeableness items, and twelve forced choices. Three blinded judges rated open
responses on five dimensions. Default stability jointly required ICC(3,k)$\geq
.70$, cross-domain and perturbation profile $\rho\geq .70$, absolute mean
perturbation $\leq .25$, and model SD$\geq .15$. Prompt steerability and convergence of
self-report/choice with open behavior were separate frozen claims.

\subsection{Models differ, but few differences recur}

The clearest recurring educational difference is how readily a model gives
direct help. Blinded ratings of help directness have high judge reliability
(ICC(3,k)=.905), recur across teaching tasks (cross-task ICC=.629; median task
$\rho=.647$), and agree with an independent behavioral criterion: responses
classified as human-like \texttt{telling} score 1.360 points more direct than
\texttt{probing}/\texttt{focus} responses. Default model means span roughly 1.65
points, from GLM at the least direct end to MiniMax-M2.7 at the most direct end.
This is the strongest evidence in the archive for a recurring educational
tendency rather than an impression from one benchmark (Figure~\ref{fig:main}A).

Expressed confidence provides a second, narrower pattern. Across four independent
sources, its model profile is stable (ICC(3,1)=.852; median task $\rho=.886$),
with Doubao highest (.977) and MiniMax-M3 lowest (.878). This pattern concerns
how firmly an answer is expressed, not whether the answer is correct. It also
does not collapse revision and abstention: Qwen combines high expressed
confidence (.956) with the highest abstention on unanswerable items (.912).

Table~\ref{tab:model-profiles} turns these results into model-level evaluations
without converting them into a quality ranking. Directness is an exact-item
centered default score; prompt movement is the mean change on the 1--5 semantic
scale across standard and hard contexts. A more direct or more steerable model is
not inherently a better tutor.

These semantic results are not reducible to response length. Semantic dimensions
identify models on held-out teaching tasks at .322, compared with .239 for length
and .167 chance. However, generic tasks are even easier to attribute (.372 with
combined transparent features versus .242 for teaching policy features), and
cross-domain policy geometry is not preserved (Mantel $r=.296$, $p=.307$).
Stable authorship cues are therefore a necessary negative control: recognizing a
model is not sufficient evidence of an educational personality.

\subsection{A shared prompt imposes a common bias, not a common tutor}

The same explicit pedagogy prompt causes a large, directionally consistent
behavioral shift. All nine models
ask more questions and produce shorter turns in both standard and hard contexts;
cross-model dispersion falls to .763 of its generic-prompt baseline (95\% CI
[.738, .794]). In blinded semantic ratings, the prompt reduces help directness
by 1.709 points in standard and 1.542 in hard contexts, increases elicitation by
2.470 and 2.171, and reduces cognitive load by .759 and .721. The shift in help
directness is about as large as the entire default-model range.

This is effective steering: a concise instruction reliably biases the
distribution of observable teaching moves. It is not personalization, because
every learner context is pushed in the same direction. Nor does it make models
interchangeable. Cross-prompt model
attribution remains .301 and .297, both above the .167 chance level. Models also
differ in prompt responsiveness after accounting for available headroom, and
some pairwise model rankings reverse after prompting. The evidence therefore
supports neither fixed personality nor complete prompt determination: models
have distinguishable defaults, a shared prompt moves all of them, and the amount
and sometimes direction of residual movement is model-specific. Prompt steering
should therefore be evaluated for each model configuration rather than treated
as a portable, model-agnostic teaching layer.

\subsection{Several intuitive personalities do not survive}

The archive contains many plausible-looking differences that fail once
recurrence, discriminant validity, or behavioral transfer is required. The most
instructive case is next-step actionability. The pedagogy prompt increases it by
1.047 points in standard and .880 in hard contexts for every model, so it is
highly steerable. Yet its cross-task ICC is $-.111$ and its median task
correlation is $-.771$: the model ordering reverses rather than recurs.
Steerability is therefore not evidence of a stable default.

Relational communion is reliably measured (ICC=.945), but correlates .805 with
surface warmth and shows no model variance in the Socratic panel. Instructional
agency remains exploratory because its frozen pilot reliability (.657) misses
the .70 threshold. Personalization and diagnostic specificity also fail
cross-task recurrence, while learner requests do not reliably change the
question/answer behaviors they request. These results contradict the intuitive
idea that warm or adaptive-sounding language demonstrates learner-responsive
teaching.

Attempts to translate generic human traits fare no better. Organizational style
recurs outside tutoring (median $\rho=.771$), but correlates negatively with
IFEval accuracy ($\rho=-.886$, exact $p=.035$), so it cannot be treated as
conscientiousness. Cautious wording does not establish honesty--humility, and a
proposed normative-permissiveness axis reverses across its two candidate tasks
($\rho=-.486$). The honest conclusion is not that these models lack all such
traits, but that the present archive does not identify them.

A targeted five-model pilot finds affiliative open behavior that transports
across educational and non-educational conflicts ($\rho=.900$) and survives one
irrelevant perturbation ($\rho=.718$). Yet IPIP self-report correlates $-.200$
with open behavior, forced choice is at ceiling, and open affiliation correlates
.900 with warmth. This is limited behavioral evidence, not Big Five
agreeableness.

\subsection{Steering a style is not personalizing instruction}

Prompting all models toward questions improves aggregate agreement with human
teacher actions: all 54 classifier--difficulty--model contrasts rise by
.091--.114 in standard and .145--.180 in hard contexts. The aggregate gain,
however, comes mainly from contexts dominated by probing. In the 48 standard
contexts where human teachers chose \texttt{telling}, generic-prompt agreement is
.394--.442 and pedagogy-prompt agreement falls to .111--.125. The inferred
telling rate drops by .234--.280. A recognizable ``Socratic'' style can thus be
consistently induced and consistently wrong for the local teaching decision
(Figure~\ref{fig:main}C).
The prompt changes what the tutor tends to do; it does not infer what this learner
needs. Aggregate improvement and contextual adaptation are different claims.

The learner-state results reveal a second separation. Across 6,000
model--history observations, teaching quality relates only weakly to diagnosis
(mean within-model Spearman=.173) and not to evidence accuracy ($-.010$). With
the exact history controlled, item-only diagnosis reaches AUC=.822; adding four
teaching dimensions yields .816, and their mean yields .817. Fluent,
adaptive-sounding teaching does not supply the missing diagnosis.

The prospective factorial explains part of this gap. Explicit system clauses
strongly control question-first and answer-reveal behavior and replicate under a
different call order. In contrast, learner requests
for direct help versus exploration change answer reveal by only .073 in the
parent panel and .066 in replication, below the frozen .10 gate; their effect on
question-first is small and opposite-signed. Models follow the system-level
teaching instruction much more reliably than the learner's expressed need.

All three operational endpoints pass every registered parent gate and the downgrade-only
order replication. Effects are positive for all five
models in the full panel, matching parent subset, and replication. By family,
question effects span .750--.787 in the parent and .750--.863 in replication;
answer effects span .878--.928 and .887--.975; encouragement-marker effects span
.525--.659 and .487--.600. Thus no one model or problem family supplies the
aggregate result.

A blinded detector audit validates question-first and answer reveal against
human labels (balanced accuracy 1.000 and .996). The encouragement detector does
not pass its semantic validation gates (balanced accuracy=.818,
$\kappa=.631$), so the third row supports only use of the frozen lexicon, not a
claim that the response is genuinely warm or supportive.

Execution is not the main unsolved problem. When externally assigned ASK or
EXPLAIN, models realize the action at .998 and 1.000. Two selectors reach only
.519 and .500 accuracy, and composing selection with realization performs worse
than a single-pass tutor ($-.058$ and $-.077$). Stable educational style mainly
describes what a model tends to do; it does not show that the model knows when to
do it.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{Recurring behavior remains conditional.}
Help directness and expressed confidence recur, but most plausible dimensions do
not, and even the recurring differences are substantially displaced by a shared
instruction. Residual model identity, unequal responsiveness, and rank reversals
show that neither a default score nor a prompt effect describes the tutor on its
own. What recurs is a model-conditioned tendency expressed under a task and an
instruction. This conditional account is also educationally important: a stable
preference for telling or eliciting describes how support is allocated, not
whether that allocation fits a particular learner. Generic authorship signatures
may likewise recur without transporting the educational construct, so model
attribution alone is insufficient.

\paragraph{A fixed shift cannot substitute for adaptation.}
The pedagogy prompt reliably changes how tutors act, but it does not establish
that the induced action is appropriate. Its average gain comes from probing
contexts, while agreement drops sharply where human teachers told. Teaching
presentation adds no learner-state signal, and near-perfect action realization
coexists with near-chance action selection. These results locate the limitation
more precisely than a generic claim that prompting ``does not work'': prompting
is effective for moving a behavioral distribution, but personalization requires
evidence about the learner and a context-conditioned choice among teaching
actions. Learning outcomes remain a further, untested requirement.

\paragraph{The tutor system is the relevant unit of evaluation.}
Our intervention varies prompts and system-level clauses inside a fixed
generation setup, not a full harness. Memory, retrieval, learner modeling,
action routing, tools, or post-generation checks could address limitations of a
fixed prompt. Yet the present results caution against treating the base model as
a neutral, interchangeable executor: the same instruction produces different
movement across models, so changing the model can change the resulting tutor
even when the surrounding logic is held constant. End-to-end evaluation should
therefore pair each intended model with its instructional setup and report
recurring defaults, induced change, model-specific responsiveness,
action-selection accuracy, and context-specific outcomes. The same discipline
applies to construct claims: inducible actionability is not stable, communion
largely redescribes warmth, organization does not validate conscientiousness,
and agreeable self-report does not predict open behavior. Consistency,
steerability, adaptation, and efficacy remain distinct targets.

\section{Conclusion}

AI tutors in this finite panel differ in educational behavior, and a small subset
of those differences recurs, but the result is not a single fixed personality
and must not be equated with human traits or pedagogical quality. A shared prompt can strongly steer every model
without making models equivalent. The central boundary is educational: steering
a tutor toward questions, restraint, or warmth does not personalize instruction.
The same fixed shift can help probing contexts and harm telling contexts, while
models that execute a prescribed action nearly perfectly still struggle to
select it from the learner context. AI-tutor evaluation should therefore target
the model--instruction--context system and keep behavioral recurrence, steering,
action selection, and learning outcomes separate.

\section*{Limitations}

The core panel contains six systems; response count does not create model
replicates, so population and family claims are excluded. Most quality outcomes
are LLM-judged, with judge swaps, expert-pair calibration, human action labels,
and human-gold diagnosis as checks. Human action agreement is not learning gain;
efficacy requires prospective learners and objective pre/post tests.

The affiliation pilot contains five configurations, twelve synthetic conflicts,
and one irrelevant perturbation. Exact profile tests are underpowered,
generator/judge families overlap, and the primary score is not discriminant from
surface warmth. Broader perturbations, behavioral games, and an independent
larger model panel are required before any general-personality claim.

The prospective factorial uses synthetic elementary-math contexts and surface
detectors. A post-result blind audit validates question-first and correct-answer
reveal, but the encouragement lexicon fails semantic warmth validation and is
reported literally. Its five endpoints are provider snapshots, and
the eight-problem replay occurred later; provider-version drift and repeat-prompt
caching cannot be separated from request-order robustness. Component effects are
not learning outcomes, and reproduced interactions rule out treating the clauses
as independent latent traits.

Provider system prompts, alias updates, and serving backends are uncontrolled.
Generation cannot be exactly repeated because most settings and all seeds and
prompt versions were not retained. Findings concern frozen deployed
configurations, not immutable weights. The semantic complete-case rule was fixed
after a provider failure but before formal analysis; its 0.56\% paired technical
attrition is fully reported.

\section*{Ethics Statement}

The study analyzes existing model responses and educational histories. Public
artifacts use hashes and derived measurements rather than raw learner histories
or source responses. External semantic coding used only explicitly authorized
endpoints and a frozen payload. Model-conditioned differences must not be read as
human traits, demographic characteristics, or evidence that one system improves
learning. Deployment should test learner outcomes and provide educator oversight,
especially where a uniform Socratic policy may withhold needed direct help.
