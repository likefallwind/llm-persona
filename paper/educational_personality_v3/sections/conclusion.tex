\section{Conclusion}

For the five deployments and educational settings studied here, recurring
teaching tendencies have prospective predictive value. Model defaults improve
forecasts of answer revelation, reasoning elicitation, and affect
acknowledgement on new problems; model-specific student-state patterns provide
an additional gain for acknowledgement, with no resolved gain for the other
two actions. Explicit teaching instructions make demanded actions converge
while leaving differences in permissible accompanying behavior. These findings
support describing educational personality as a deployment's recurring,
prompt-dependent action profile. They do not support immutable human-like
types, universal differences between all model pairs, or claims about learning
benefits. Content-screened subsets preserve the predictive pattern, while
limited natural-error agreement prevents treating screening as a guarantee
of correctness.
